A pruned graph that hides the answer

Graph indexes such as DiskANN keep each node's neighbor list short with a rule called RobustPrune. Here are L + 3 points on a sphere where that rule leaves the best answer to the query connected to a single point, a blocker the query scores below zero. Beam search of width L fills up with decoys and never reaches the answer.

This is the exact construction proved in Lean, in an idealized model: no degree cap, every other point is a pruning candidate, one pruning pass, search from a fixed start. It is a worst case, not a measurement of real indexes.

The proof covers α from 1 to 1.5. Above 1.5 the page still computes the prune exactly, but the theorem says nothing.

Why it breaks

RobustPrune visits a node's candidates from nearest to farthest. It keeps a candidate p and drops every later candidate v with α·|p − v| ≤ |u − v|: if p is much closer to v, the search can reach v through p. The blocker p* = (16, 24, −3)/29 sits between the decoys and the winner w = (−2, 6, 3)/7 in distance. From the start and every decoy, w is pruned, by p* or by a nearer decoy; from w, p* prunes every decoy. The winner keeps one edge, to p*.

That shortcut assumes the query wants what is near p*. Here the query points straight up. The winner leads every other point by more than 0.40 in cosine, but p* scores below zero, below every decoy. The beam keeps the L best points it has seen, so once it holds the L decoys it never expands p*, and w is never reached.

Turn on the conic-hull edge to keep the edge from the top decoy to w. Every direction from that decoy toward the other points lowers the query score, while the direction to w raises it, so no combination of kept edges can stand in for it. A pruning rule that keeps an edge unless it lies in the cone of the kept ones must keep this one, and the search then finds w.

The decoys all lie within 0.025 of the start; the picture spreads them out so they can be told apart. All pruning and search use the exact coordinates.
