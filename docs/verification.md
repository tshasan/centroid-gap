# Verification

Everything in `IndexRecall/` and `Lloyd.lean` builds with the pinned Lean and Mathlib 4.24.0. The axiom audit walks every theorem declaration in those modules, including private and Lean-generated ones: **1,323 declarations, 588 written by hand and 735 generated by Lean** (equation lemmas, structure projections, auxiliary proofs). It fails on any project axiom, on any axiom beyond `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and if a theorem cited in the docs is missing. The count was last rechecked on 26 November 2026 on x86-64 Linux (`lake build` about 30 s with a warm Mathlib cache, audit about 2.5 min). [Axiom audit](../checks/AuditAxioms.lean)

The count rose as results were added: 1,031 at the time of the x86 replication below, 1,156 with the one-vector theorems, 1,315 with their tightness witnesses and planar variant, and 1,323 with the planar power router.

## Numerical checks and their limits

A standalone Python program constructs the records, recomputes means, finds each nearest neighbor by full search, and examines every probe prefix with exact arithmetic. It checked 20 interval queries across four scalar instances and all 109 queries of five planar instances. For 4,096 planar records the costs were **2,176 versus 128** for every one of the 32 queries. Changing only the representatives also cost 128. [Exact checks](../checks/check_ivf_gap.py)

Separate optimizations checked uniqueness without assuming the constructed partition. On the line these covered every split for two lists on 8,194 records, and a sorted-prefix dynamic program for three lists on 27,651 records. In the plane, branch-and-bound over unlabeled partitions of 16 records into at most four lists gave the unique optimum at SSE **389/64**. Larger numerical cases check search behavior. The Lean optimality proofs cover all parameter values.

**Moving the queries.** The planar gap survives every displacement $|\Delta q_x|\le L/24$ and $|\Delta q_y|\le H/4$ with the data fixed. In the construction's rank table, the original absolute horizontal offset from the corresponding representative or tip is between $L/12$ and $2L/3$. After the shift it stays strictly between zero and $L$. Spacing $3L$ then keeps the same closest member of each type and excludes ties within a type. All horizontal coordinates remain in $[0,2Lk]$, so the squared horizontal cost varies by at most $H/4$. Across types, a height advantage of at least $1/2$ with height sum at most $3$ gives a squared vertical advantage of at least $(2(3H/4)-3)/2>H/4$. The nearest answer and the three owner ranks therefore persist throughout the rectangle. This is a paper corollary. The independent program also checks all four corners for all eight queries at $k=16$. [Construction](../paper/main.pdf)

The planar data points can move as well. For each fixed $k$ there are finitely many unlabeled partitions. Each partition's SSE at its own means is continuous in the data coordinates, and the unique optimum has a strict cost margin over every competitor. The finitely many strict score comparisons that determine the true neighbors and router ranks are continuous in both the records and the queries. Their margins and the SSE margins therefore stay positive in some open neighborhood of the entire instance. This paper argument gives a data-perturbation radius that may depend on $k$.

**Finite precision matters.** At 4,096 planar records, computing squared Euclidean distances directly in binary32 lost the unique nearest answer on all 32 queries. The large query height makes rounding erase score differences. Evaluating the algebraically equivalent score $(x-q_x)^2+y^2-2q_y y$, which drops the common $q_y^2$, recovered all 32 unique answers and all 96 tie-free router outcomes at their predicted ranks. Direct binary64 also passed this instance. The test rounds the exact representatives to the chosen format, then rounds every scoring operation. The frequency and size of the gap on real embeddings remain open.

A separate Python script checks the graph-pruning and micro-shard theorems: it verifies the exact rational `RobustPrune` isolation and beam-search failure of Theorem A1 for $L \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ and $\alpha \in [1, 3/2]$, checks the explicit Farkas witness for Theorem A2, and runs 12,000 greedy tip-scored micro-shard searches against the Theorem B3 $(\varepsilon+\eta)\|q\|$ bound. [Graph and conic checks](../checks/verify_graph_conic_theorems.py)

## Reproducing the checks

From the project root with Lake on the path, run the following commands.

```sh
lake build
lake env lean checks/AuditAxioms.lean
python3 checks/check_ivf_gap.py
python3 checks/verify_graph_conic_theorems.py
```

For an isolated release pair, set `version` to one of the table entries. This uses elan's toolchain selection and the release's locked package revisions.

```sh
version=4.36.0
work=$(mktemp -d)
cp -R IndexRecall IndexRecall.lean Lloyd.lean "$work/"
cp checks/AuditAxioms.lean "$work/"
sed "s/v4.24.0/v$version/g" lakefile.toml > "$work/lakefile.toml"
printf 'leanprover/lean4:v%s\n' "$version" > "$work/lean-toolchain"
(
  set -e
  cd "$work"
  export MATHLIB_NO_CACHE_ON_UPDATE=1
  lake update
  awk '$1 == "import" && $2 ~ /^Mathlib\./ {print $2}' ./*.lean IndexRecall/*.lean |
    sort -u | xargs lake exe cache get
  lake build
  lake env lean AuditAxioms.lean
)
```

For a Rust replay, build the pinned exporter with Lean 4.24.0 and Nanoda with `cargo build --release --locked`. Run the following command from the built 4.24.0 project.

```sh
python3 checks/check_export.py /path/to/lean4export /path/to/nanoda_bin
```

## x86-64 Faiss replication

This section is self-contained. It records a rerun of the million-vector benchmark ([commands](../experiments/README.md#million-vector-routing)) and of the cosine experiment ([commands](../experiments/README.md#large-margin-cosine-misses)) on x86-64 Linux. The published numbers came from an Apple Silicon Mac. Environment: Python 3.12, NumPy 2.5.4, CPU Faiss 1.12.0 (the pinned wheel), Ryzen 5 7600X, four Faiss threads. The data were restaged with `experiments/ivf_workload_data.py` and every staged `.npy` matched the SHA-256 recorded in its manifest. The commands were the ones in `experiments/README.md`, unchanged. Outputs are in [results/replication-x86/](../results/replication-x86/) (`share/deep.json`, `share/t2i.json` from the `export` stage, `cosine-margin/`). [experiments/compare_replication.py](../experiments/compare_replication.py) compares two result JSON files value by value.

**What matched exactly.**

- The data: every staged input array hash, and every retained query count after margin filtering (DEEP 2,988 validation, 4,977 test, 16,317 fit; Text-to-Image 2,977, 4,962, 16,243 and 1,993 image controls).
- The cosine experiment's headline result. All 5,000 queries per corpus were eligible. 30 DEEP and 5 Text-to-Image queries qualified at Δ ≥ 0.09. Misses at probes 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 were DEEP 12, 8, 4, 3, 2, 2, 0 and Text-to-Image 4, 3, 2, 1, 0, 0, 0, the same as `results/cosine-margin/`. Every query's qualifying flag, winner ID, runner-up ID and owner-list rank were identical in both CSVs.
- The oracle's 95% recall@10 probe count (6 probes on both corpora), the probe counts at 95% recall@1 (14 on DEEP, 30 on Text-to-Image), and the DEEP centroid recall@10 probe count (18).
- The Text-to-Image cosine index: identical frozen owner hash and identical list sizes (321 to 3,431).
- Rebuilding the index a second time on x86 gave identical owner arrays on both corpora, so the x86 run is self-consistent.

**What differed.** The counts did not match exactly. The k-means step in Faiss is not bit-reproducible between this machine and the Mac, so the lists differ slightly, and everything that depends on membership shifts by a small amount.

- Index list sizes (`results/*.json`, `index`): DEEP min and max 152 and 3,634 published versus 152 and 3,633 here. Text-to-Image 316 and 3,786 versus 309 and 3,798.
- DEEP centroid, test, recall@1 at the 14-probe budget: recall 0.951979 published, 0.951376 here. Mean documents scanned 14,873.38 versus 14,870.68. Median 14,324 versus 14,320.
- DEEP centroid, test, recall@10 at 18 probes: recall 0.952481 versus 0.952100. Mean documents scanned 18,931.61 versus 18,924.45. Median 18,366 versus 18,348.
- Text-to-Image centroid, test, recall@10: 34 probes published, **35 probes here** to reach 95% (recall 0.950262 at 34 probes published versus 0.951350 at 35 here). Mean documents scanned 33,529.01 versus 34,495.13. The published index crossed 95% one probe earlier, which is a one-probe shift at a threshold crossing, not a change in the trend.
- Text-to-Image centroid, test, recall@1 at 30 probes: recall 0.950020 versus 0.950423. Mean documents scanned 29,612.59 versus 29,600.49.
- Counts of differing leaf values by section (value by value, no timing fields exist in these files): DEEP `flat-baseline` 172, `flat-fitted` 232, `pq` 1,012 of 1,750 router entries; Text-to-Image `flat-baseline` 116, `flat-fitted` 246, `pq` 781, `shift` 364. Most are the same small shifts in recall and scan counts, propagated through percentiles and means.
- Cosine experiment, DEEP: the 30 qualifying queries and all 35,000 query-budget miss decisions are unchanged, but the DEEP spherical index differs in membership (different `source_owner_sha256_le_i4` and mean hash). Documents scanned differ for 14 of 5,000 queries at one probe, so for example total scored documents among eligible queries at one probe are 5,812,987 versus 5,812,981 published. Cosines and leads differ in the last bit (at most 2.2e-16 for winner cosine and 4.4e-16 for lead) because of float64 summation order.
- Cosine experiment, Text-to-Image: the CSV differs only in those last-bit cosine values (at most 3.3e-16 in lead), so the summary differs only in fields that depend on them, not in any count.

So the qualitative findings reproduce on x86, and the cosine miss counts reproduce exactly. The exact work counts at the 95% crossings do not reproduce byte for byte, because the clustering differs slightly on this hardware. Not done here: timing comparison, and any other Faiss version or thread count.
